AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Hollister, CA

A clogged condensate line or overflowing drain pan can stain ceilings and walls for weeks before anyone notices.

Call (831) 271-0459

AC leak water damage starts with condensation. A cooling system pulls water out of the air, and that water is supposed to drain away through a small line. When the line clogs or the pan overflows, water spills into the attic, the closet or the wall around the air handler, and the first sign is usually a stain.

This is not usually an emergency by the minute, but it is urgent by the week. Turn off the system if water is dripping, and keep people out from under a sagging ceiling. We dry and repair the water damage, and an HVAC technician fixes the drain, pan or unit.

Symptoms of an AC leak

  • A yellow or brown stain on a ceiling near an air handler, vent or closet.
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near an indoor unit.
  • A musty smell near the air handler, or damp insulation in the attic.
  • The system shutting off by itself, often from a float switch in the pan.
  • Rust streaks or drips on a wall below a ductless mini-split indoor unit.

Where AC water goes

In many homes the air handler sits in an attic or a closet. Water that overflows a pan runs onto insulation, soaks the ceiling drywall below, and then stains the room underneath. With a ductless unit the water may run down the wall behind it and wick into the baseboard.

Because the water is typically clean condensate, it often leaves the structure in good shape if the leak is caught early. Left alone, it can feed odor and staining in insulation and drywall, which is why we treat the area and dry it rather than just painting.

Why this happens more in warm, dry valleys

Systems that run for long cooling seasons make a lot of condensate, and that is true of inland California valleys like the one Hollister sits in. A drain line that works in spring may clog by midsummer from algae and dust. A yearly flush of the line by an HVAC technician is a simple habit that avoids most of these calls.

If the leak reached a wall or flooring, you may also want to read about wall and drywall water damage repair. For a larger water event, see water extraction.

What to have ready when you call

  • Where the stain or drip is and how long it has been there.
  • Whether the system is a central unit, an attic air handler or a ductless mini-split.
  • Whether the unit is still running, and whether the pan has water in it.
  • Whether an HVAC technician has looked at the drain line.
  • Whether the stain grows when the system runs.

If the system is still running while water is dripping, switch it off and call your HVAC company. Running a unit with a blocked drain keeps feeding water into the structure.

A few habits that prevent repeat leaks

  • Have a technician flush the condensate line once a year before the cooling season.
  • Replace a rusted drain pan before it fails.
  • Consider a float switch that shuts the system off if the pan fills.

What affects AC leak repair cost

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • How long it leaked. Weeks of leaking reach more insulation and drywall than a single overflow.
  • Location of the unit. Attic leaks tend to wet ceilings below, closet leaks wet flooring.
  • Materials affected. Insulation, drywall and carpet each have their own steps.
  • Drying days. Equipment time depends on readings.
  • Finish work. Texture, primer and paint follow the dry-out.

Safety first and the standard we reference

ANSI/IICRC S500 is our reference for drying goals once a condensate leak has wet ceiling or wall materials. It is a published standard, not a credential claim.

  • Switch the system off at the thermostat.
  • Keep power off to wet light fixtures.
  • Do not climb into the attic if the framing is wet.

Questions about AC Leak Water Damage in Hollister

How do I know if a stain is from my air conditioner?

Look for a stain near an air handler, vent or closet that appears or grows during cooling season. A moisture meter and an HVAC technician can confirm.

Can AC water damage lead to odor?

Yes, because wet insulation and drywall can smell musty if left damp. Drying and removing wet material solves that, and the drain needs to be fixed.

Do I need an HVAC technician as well?

Yes. We dry and repair the damage, and an HVAC technician clears the drain, replaces a rusted pan or services the unit.

Is an AC leak covered by insurance?

Sometimes, if the failure was sudden. Long-term leaks and maintenance issues often are not. Ask your insurer.
