Bubbled Paint and Water Damage on Walls: A Hollister, CA Guide

Blisters, peeling and stains in paint are signs. Here is what they point to and what to do.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

Bubbled paint is a symptom, not a problem on its own. When moisture gets behind paint, the paint lifts and forms blisters, which can stay soft or crack open. The question to answer is where the moisture came from and whether it is still there.

What bubbled paint usually means

What you seeLikely causeFirst check
Round blisters on a bathroom wallSteam and poor ventilationIs there an exhaust fan, and does it run?
Bubbles with a brown ringWater from a leak behind or aboveLook for a source above the area
Peeling near the floorMoisture rising from a wet baseboard or slabCheck for leaks near the floor and outside drainage
Blisters on an outside wallWater through siding, windows or roofInspect flashing and caulk

A stain that grows, a soft wall or a musty smell means moisture is active. Paint that bubbled once during a humid week and is stable may only need a dry-out and repainting. When in doubt, check with a moisture meter before you paint over anything.

What to do before you repaint

  1. Find the source. Look for leaks, condensation or damp outside soil near the wall.
  2. Stop the moisture. A plumber or roofer may be needed.
  3. Let the wall dry. Use a moisture meter to confirm that drywall and framing are dry.
  4. Scrape loose paint, prime with a stain-blocking primer and repaint.
  5. Watch the spot for a few weeks and call if the stain comes back.

A note on older paint

Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. Do not sand or scrape old paint without learning the safe practices first, and ask a qualified professional if you are not sure. This is general safety guidance, not a diagnosis of your home.

When bubbled paint means you should call

If the wall is soft, the stain is growing or the paint bubbles keep coming back, moisture may be inside the wall. A crew can map it with meters and thermal imaging, and tell you whether drying or cutting out drywall is the right move. Hollister homes of different ages

Older homes near downtown often have layers of paint on plaster, which can blister differently than new drywall. Newer homes in subdivisions may show blisters near bathrooms where ventilation is weak. If you live near Tres Pinos or San Juan Bautista, the same checks apply, though older rural buildings may have more exposed siding and roofs.

Paint problems that are not water damage

Not every blister is a leak. Paint can bubble because of poor surface preparation, painting over dust or grease, applying oil-based paint over latex without priming, or painting in high heat. The test is whether the area is damp or stained, and whether the problem returns after a proper repaint.

Possible causeTypical lookNot a leak if
Humidity during dryingSmall blisters across a whole wallThe wall is dry and cool and no stain is present
Poor surface prepPeeling in sheets from a smooth surfaceThe paint lifts clean with no moisture behind
Steam in a bathroomBlisters near the shower or ceilingA fan fixes the problem
Real water intrusionBrown rings, soft drywall, musty smellThis is a leak

Questions to ask yourself

  • Is the wall on the other side of a bathroom, kitchen or roof?
  • Did it rain or did someone use a shower before the paint changed?
  • Does the area feel cool or damp compared to the rest of the wall?
  • Is there a smell of mildew near the spot?
  • Has the spot grown over a few days?

Answering yes to several of these means the moisture may be active and worth checking. A simple moisture meter, available at hardware stores or used by a crew, can show the difference between a dry paint problem and a wet wall.

Repainting the right way

Once the wall is dry, remove loose paint, prime with a stain-blocking primer and use paint rated for the room, such as a bathroom paint where steam is common. Skipping the primer is the most common reason a stain bleeds through new paint a month later.

A local and safety note

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency says that homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. For Hollister homeowners with older houses, that means asking a qualified professional about safe practices before disturbing paint on walls and trim.

For building or permit questions on bigger repairs, City of Hollister Development Services handles properties inside city limits, and the San Benito County Resource Management Agency handles unincorporated areas.

Questions homeowners ask

Is bubbled paint always water damage?

No. It can also be caused by humidity, poor surface prep or paint applied over a dirty surface. A moisture check helps separate them.

Can I just paint over bubbled paint?

Not until the cause is fixed and the wall is dry. Painting over moisture traps it and the blisters return.

How do I tell if the wall behind is wet?

Press gently on the area, look for stains and smell for mustiness. A moisture meter gives a clear answer.

Do old Hollister houses need special care when repainting?

Homes built before 1978 may have lead-based paint, according to the EPA, so ask a qualified professional about safe practices before sanding or scraping.
