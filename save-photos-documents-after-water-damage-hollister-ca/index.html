How to Save Photos and Important Documents After Water Damage in Hollister, CA

Act fast, handle gently and dry slowly. A short guide to rescuing what matters most.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

Photos and papers are often the things people care about most after water damage, and they are also the most fragile. Paper starts to stick, ink starts to run and mold can start within a couple of days. A calm, quick process gives you the best chance of saving them.

First steps

  1. Make sure the area is safe before you go in.
  2. Wear gloves if the water might be contaminated.
  3. Handle wet items by the edges and lift them with support underneath.
  4. Photograph valuable items in place before moving them.
  5. Move items out of the wet area and keep them flat when possible.

Do not try to separate stuck photos or pages by pulling. Soak a stuck stack in clean cool water if you must, and slide the pieces apart gently under water. If you cannot do it safely, freeze the stack and ask a conservator.

Drying photos

Rinse mud from photos with clean cool water, and lay them face up on a clean absorbent surface such as a paper towel, a clean cloth or a screen. Do not stack them while wet. Keep them out of direct sunlight, which can fade and curl them, and use a fan with gentle airflow in a clean room.

Framed photos stuck to glass should be left in the frame and frozen if you cannot dry them quickly. Old, antique or one-of-a-kind photos may need a professional conservator, so freeze them and ask for advice before you attempt any cleaning.

Freezing stops mold and gives you time. Put wet papers in freezer bags with wax paper between layers and freeze them until you can handle them. A freezer is a pause button, not a cure, and it buys time for sorting.

Protecting records next time

  • Scan or photograph important papers and store copies in the cloud.
  • Keep originals in a waterproof, fire-rated box or a safe deposit box.
  • Keep a photo or video inventory of your home and belongings.
  • Store boxes off the floor, especially in garages and lower levels.
  • Back up photos on more than one device.

Let the structure dry while you rescue what matters

The best time to rescue papers is while the crew is drying the room, because the space is safe and dry air helps. If your papers were in a wall or ceiling that got wet, our structural drying team can explain how long the area will stay damp. Whether you are in Tres Pinos, San Juan Bautista or Hollister itself, the steps are the same. Ask your insurer which documents you need for a claim, and keep those in a separate, dry folder.

Common mistakes with wet paper and photos

  • Peeling stuck photos apart dry, which tears the emulsion.
  • Using a hair dryer or oven, which warps and bakes the paper.
  • Leaving wet items in a closed box or bag, which invites mold.
  • Scrubbing the surface of a photo, which removes the image.
  • Waiting more than a day or two to freeze or dry them.

Mold on paper can start within a couple of days in warm, humid conditions, so the first day or two matters most. Even a rough rinse and a plan to freeze is better than leaving a soaked box on the floor.

Digital copies and what to do now

Once photos are dry, scan them at a high resolution and store copies in two places. A scan also gives you a copy for any restoration work on fragile or faded originals. Label the files with names and dates while you remember who is in the pictures.

If your hard drive or camera card was wet, do not plug it in. Rinse off mud with clean water, let it dry in open air and ask a data recovery professional before you try to read it. Plugging in a wet drive can destroy data that could otherwise be recovered.

A simple home setup that protects papers

A small fire-rated and water-resistant box on a shelf, not the floor, protects the few papers that cannot be replaced. Put a card inside listing what it holds, and keep a second copy of that list with a relative or in the cloud.

When to call a conservator

For one of a kind items such as antique photographs, old documents, art on paper or family Bibles, a professional conservator is worth consulting. Freeze the item first so that mold and damage stop, then ask what is possible. Conservators can often do things that are not possible at home, such as separating stuck pages safely.

A practical record-keeping tip from the state

The California Department of Insurance recommends video-recording your belongings room by room before a loss and storing the recording somewhere separate, such as a safe deposit box. It also advises listing damaged items with brand names and model numbers and keeping old receipts and bills to establish price and age.

For Hollister residents, that means a short video walkthrough now, stored away from the house, can save a great deal of time if water damage ever happens.

Source type: California Department of Insurance consumer guide.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I save wet family photos?

Often, if you act quickly. Rinse, lay them flat and dry them gently, or freeze them until you can deal with them.

Should I use a hair dryer or the oven?

No. Heat can warp photos and paper. Use gentle airflow at room temperature.

What if documents are muddy?

Rinse gently with clean cool water and dry flat. Freeze valuable ones if you cannot handle them right away.

Where should Hollister homeowners keep copies of important records?

The California Department of Insurance suggests storing a video of your belongings in a separate place such as a safe deposit box. Keep scans of papers in the cloud as well.
