If water is standing on your floor right now, treat it as an emergency, because every hour of soaking pushes moisture deeper into drywall, subfloor and framing. Our dispatch line answers 24/7, including nights and weekends. Water extraction is the first physical step of a water loss: pumps and extraction units remove the standing water so drying can begin.

While you wait for the crew, shut off the main water valve if the source is still running, lift valuables and small furniture off the floor, and keep children and pets out of the water. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water. If the water is near outlets or appliances, call the utility or 911 before calling us.

When standing water is an emergency

Water that has been sitting for more than a day is no longer a simple cleanup. Carpet pad holds water like a sponge, baseboards wick moisture upward, and wet insulation stays wet long after the surface looks fine. Calling early matters here, because extraction done early often saves flooring that would otherwise be removed.

The source decides how serious the job is. Clean water from a supply line is the easiest case. Water that came up from a floor drain, a toilet or a sewer line is contaminated and needs the safety steps on our sewage backup cleanup page. Rain or groundwater that entered through a door or foundation falls under flood damage cleanup.

How water extraction is done

1

Find the source and the extent

The crew checks that the water supply is off, then maps how far the water has traveled using moisture meters and, where useful, a thermal imaging camera.

2

Remove standing water

Submersible pumps handle depth. Truck-mounted or portable extraction units and wet vacuums handle what pumps leave behind, including water trapped in carpet and pad.

3

Lift what cannot be saved

Soaked pad and loose wet materials come out so they stop feeding moisture into the structure. Salvageable carpet may be lifted and dried separately.

4

Hand off to drying

Extraction removes liquid water. Air movers and dehumidifiers then pull out the moisture that remains, which is the work described on our structural drying page.

Hollister homes and the water they get

Hollister sits in the San Benito Valley, and the water calls that come from it vary a lot. If your house has a raised floor and crawl space, water can hide under the floor while the room above looks only damp. On a slab foundation, water tends to show at once, spreading across tile and laminate. Either way, tell dispatch which kind of foundation you have.

If your property is outside city limits, building and permit questions go to the San Benito County Resource Management Agency. Inside city limits they go to City of Hollister Development Services. Extraction itself does not normally need a permit, but any rebuild work afterward might, so ask the right office before you commit to repairs.

What to have ready when you call

  • Where the water came from and whether it has been stopped.
  • Which rooms are wet and roughly how deep the water is.
  • Whether the floors are carpet, tile, wood or laminate.
  • Whether the water might be contaminated, for example from a toilet or outdoors.
  • Whether the home is on a slab or has a raised floor with a crawl space.

Those answers let dispatch decide what equipment to send. A shallow spill on tile needs wet vacuums. Several inches over carpet in two rooms needs a pump, an extraction unit and a plan for lifting pad.

What affects the cost of extraction

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Water category. Clean, gray and contaminated water are handled differently, and contaminated water adds safety steps.
  • Depth and area. More standing water and more affected rooms mean more pump and extraction time.
  • Materials involved. Carpet and pad, hardwood, tile and subfloor each respond differently to soaking.
  • Drying that follows. Equipment count and drying days are usually the largest part of the total.
  • Rebuild scope. Removed drywall, pad or flooring may need to be replaced afterward.

Insurance in general terms: sudden and accidental water damage is often covered by a homeowners policy, while slow leaks from neglect often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Safety first and the standard we reference

ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, is the reference for how standing water is categorized and removed. We cite it as a reference, not as a claim about any certification.

  • Do not walk through standing water near outlets or cords.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on water.
  • Call 911 or the electric utility for any shock or sparking hazard.

Questions about Water Extraction in Hollister

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Hollister?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, and we confirm availability for your address on the call. Call as soon as you find water so extraction can start before it spreads.

While you wait, stop the source if you can, lift valuables, and stay out of water near outlets or appliances.

How long does water extraction take?

Most extraction visits take a few hours, depending on depth, area and how much water is trapped in carpet or under flooring. Drying afterward takes days, and the crew can explain the plan once they see the space.

Can I just use a shop vacuum myself?

For a small spill on a hard floor, yes. For anything that reached carpet, pad, walls or electrical items, a household vacuum leaves too much moisture behind and is not designed for deep water.

Will my carpet be saved?

Often the carpet can be dried if the water was clean and extraction started quickly. The pad is usually replaced, and contaminated water means the carpet goes too.
