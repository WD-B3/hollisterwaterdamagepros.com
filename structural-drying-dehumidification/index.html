Moisture mapping, air movers and dehumidifiers that dry walls, floors and framing until readings say they are dry.

Walls and floors still feel damp days after a leak?

If a leak was fixed and the wall, floor or carpet still feels cool, damp or smells musty, the water is still in the building materials. Surface towels do not reach it. The process that does is called structural drying, which uses meters to find moisture and equipment to move it out of the structure.

Structural drying is the technical name for removing moisture from walls, floors, subfloors and framing after water has soaked in. Water mitigation is the broader term for stopping the damage from getting worse, and drying is the central step of it. Dispatch answers 24/7 if you are not sure whether your space is dry.

How structural drying works

Drying is controlled evaporation. Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces, which lifts moisture into the air. Dehumidifiers then pull that moisture out of the air so it cannot settle somewhere else. The crew adjusts equipment as readings change.

1

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras trace wet areas in walls, floors and ceilings, so equipment goes where moisture is and not just where it shows.

2

Equipment placement

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are positioned by the size of the space and the class of the water loss.

3

Daily monitoring

Readings are taken at each visit and recorded in a drying log. Equipment is moved or removed as materials reach their target.

4

Final verification

A last set of readings against unaffected reference materials confirms that the structure is dry before rebuild starts.

The crew places and monitors the equipment, and we do not promise a drying time before seeing the space. Most structural drying takes several days, and the number depends on materials, air conditions and how long the water sat.

Water classes and why they matter

The industry standard for water damage restoration is ANSI/IICRC S500, and it groups water losses by how much moisture a space holds and how fast it will evaporate. Class 1 is low moisture and Class 4 involves materials that hold water deeply, such as hardwood and concrete. You can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

Class matters because it tells the crew how much equipment to use. A small wet corner of a carpeted room needs little. A flooded room with wet subfloor needs far more. If you are comparing quotes, ask how many air movers and dehumidifiers the plan includes and why.

Water mitigation in the first 24 to 72 hours

Materials start to degrade quickly when wet, and most of the damage prevention comes from acting early. Water mitigation means extracting water, drying the structure and removing materials that cannot be saved. It sits between the emergency and the rebuild, and the quality of it decides whether you replace a few feet of drywall or the whole wall.

If your loss includes standing water, extraction comes first, as covered on water extraction. If the damage started at a ceiling, see ceiling water damage repair for the repair side. For floors, carpet water damage restoration and hardwood floor water damage repair explain how each material dries.

Drying in the dry months and the wet months

Central California air is usually drier in summer and more humid in winter storms, and humidity affects how fast materials dry. The National Weather Service forecasts for the area can tell you what conditions to expect. A good drying plan adjusts equipment to indoor conditions, which is why readings matter more than a fixed number of days.

What to have ready when you call

  • When the water event happened and whether it was stopped.
  • Which rooms and materials were wet.
  • Whether standing water has already been removed.
  • Whether anything smells musty or looks stained.
  • Any readings or photographs you already have.

Drying works best when it starts early, but it also works days later if the materials are still wet. A meter reading is the best answer to whether a space needs more time.

What affects structural drying cost

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Water class and category. Higher classes and contaminated water need more equipment and care.
  • Affected area. More square feet and more rooms mean more air movers and dehumidifiers.
  • Materials. Hardwood, concrete and plaster dry slowly, drywall dries faster.
  • Days on site. Equipment time and monitoring visits depend on readings.
  • Removal needs. Materials that cannot dry are removed, and rebuild follows.

Safety first and the standard we reference

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard that describes drying goals and moisture monitoring. We use it as a reference when we set and check targets.

  • Do not turn off drying equipment early.
  • Keep doors and windows closed while dehumidifiers run.
  • Report any new odor or stain.

Questions about Structural Drying in Hollister

How do I know if my walls are still wet after a leak?

Surface touch is unreliable. Moisture meters and thermal imaging show whether drywall and framing still hold water. Call dispatch and we can explain what to check.

How long does structural drying take?

Most losses take several days, depending on the materials, the amount of water and the indoor air. The crew confirms with readings rather than a fixed number of days.

Can I just run a household dehumidifier?

For a small damp room, it can help. For soaked walls or subfloor, it cannot match the air movement and capacity of professional equipment.

What is the difference between drying and water mitigation?

Mitigation is the whole effort to stop damage from spreading, including extraction and removal. Drying is the main step inside it.
