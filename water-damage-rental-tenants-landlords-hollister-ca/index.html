Water Damage in a Rental: Tenant and Landlord Responsibilities in Hollister, CA

When water damages a rental, the first call matters. Here is who does what, in plain terms.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

Water damage in a rental raises questions that a house owner never faces. Who calls for help, who pays, and what happens to the tenant's belongings? The answers depend on the lease and on state law, so this guide covers the practical sequence, not legal advice.

First steps for tenants

  1. Make the area safe and, if you can, stop the water at the fixture shutoff.
  2. Notify the landlord or property manager right away, and put it in writing.
  3. Photograph the damage and your belongings.
  4. Move valuables out of the wet area.
  5. Keep copies of every message.

Do not wait to see if it dries on its own. Moisture spreads, and a delay can make the damage worse and the dispute harder. A written notice with the date is the single most useful piece of evidence for a tenant.

Who typically handles what

ItemUsually handled by
Structure, plumbing and building repairsLandlord, through the landlord's insurance
Tenant's belongingsTenant, through renters insurance if they have it
Emergency drying of the unitLandlord or property manager arranges it
Temporary housingDepends on the lease, the cause and the unit

A lease can change these defaults, and the cause of the loss matters. Water that came from a failed pipe is different from water that a tenant caused. Both parties are better off with a clear written record.

Tips for landlords and managers

  • Respond quickly and in writing.
  • Start drying early so damage does not spread to neighboring units.
  • Keep the scope, invoices and drying records.
  • Tell your insurer promptly.
  • Give tenants clear information on access and timing.

Neighboring units matter in multi-unit buildings, because a leak can travel through walls and ceilings. See commercial water damage restoration for larger buildings and ceiling water damage repair for a leak that came from the unit above.

Rentals in Tres Pinos, San Juan Bautista and Hollister follow California rules, but the details depend on the lease. If you are unsure of your rights, ask a tenant resource or an attorney.

A written notice checklist for tenants

  1. Your name, address and unit number.
  2. The date and time you found the water.
  3. A short description of where it is and how much.
  4. Photographs attached.
  5. A request for a repair date and a reply in writing.

Send it by text or email so there is a time stamp, and follow up with a call. Keep your own copy. If the problem gets worse, send a second notice that references the first.

Renters insurance basics

A renters policy usually covers the tenant's belongings and sometimes extra living costs, but not the building. Read yours before you need it, and note the deductible and any limits for specific items. Take a video of your belongings while everything is dry.

If you do not have renters insurance, document the damage anyway. Photos, receipts and a written list of what was lost can support a conversation with the landlord about next steps.

Keeping the peace while repairs happen

Water damage in a rental can be stressful for both sides. Tenants may be without a kitchen or a bedroom, and landlords face costs and scheduling. Clear, polite, written updates about when work will happen and what is expected reduce conflict.

If the unit cannot be lived in during repairs, ask the landlord what the lease says about rent and temporary housing, and keep receipts for extra costs. If you cannot agree, a tenant resource or attorney can explain your options. Our role is to dry the structure, not to settle those questions.

A state resource for tenants and landlords

The California Attorney General publishes a guide on residential tenants' rights that covers habitability and repairs, including the landlord's duty to keep a rental in livable condition. It is a good place to start if you are unsure of your rights.

We do not give legal advice. If there is a dispute over responsibility or deposits, ask a tenant resource, a legal aid office or an attorney.

Questions homeowners ask

Who pays for water damage in a rental?

It depends on the cause and the lease. A landlord generally handles the building, and a tenant's belongings are usually covered by the tenant's renters insurance.

Should a tenant call a restoration company?

Notify the landlord first. The landlord usually arranges the structural work.

What if the landlord does not respond?

Document your notices and ask a tenant resource or attorney about your options.

Where can Hollister renters read about habitability rules?

The California Attorney General publishes a residential tenants' rights guide. Ask an attorney for advice on your situation.
