How to Read a Water Damage Scope of Work in Hollister, CA

The scope is the document that decides what you get. Here is how to read it before you sign.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

A scope of work is the written list of what a company will do, where and with what materials. It is the document an insurer reviews and the one you rely on if there is a disagreement later. Reading it carefully takes ten minutes and can save weeks of confusion.

The parts of a scope

PartWhat to look for
HeaderYour name, the service address, the date of loss and the source of water
AreasEach room or area named, with approximate size
ActionsVerbs such as extract, remove, dry, clean, dispose and monitor
QuantitiesSquare feet, linear feet, number of units and days
ExclusionsWhat is not included, such as rebuild, permits or plumbing
Sign-offWho approves changes and how

The actions matter most. Removal of drywall to a stated height is not the same as removal of drywall to the ceiling, and the difference shows up in the total. Look for specific heights, depths and materials.

Red flags in a scope

  • Vague wording such as "as needed" with no limit.
  • Lump sums with no quantities.
  • No mention of drying equipment or monitoring.
  • No exclusions listed at all.
  • A scope that changes between the phone call and the signing.

A short scope is not necessarily a bad one, but each of these makes it hard to compare with another quote or to check later. If something is unclear, ask the company to write it out before you sign.

Change orders and hidden damage

Water damage often shows more once materials are opened. A good scope says how changes are handled: the crew documents the finding, tells you, and gets approval before extra work. Ask whether additional work needs your written approval, and whether your insurer will be told.

For the drying part of a scope, see structural drying to understand what monitoring days and equipment mean. If your scope includes removal of materials in a wall, wall and drywall water damage repair explains the steps.

Tell the company what you know

A scope is only as good as the information behind it. Share the water source, the time the loss began and anything that was moved. Properties in Tres Pinos and San Juan Bautista may involve well or septic systems, so mention those. The more accurate the facts, the fewer changes later.

Units and abbreviations you may see

TermWhat it usually means
SFSquare feet, used for floors and walls
LFLinear feet, used for baseboard and drywall cuts
EAEach, a single item or piece of equipment
DayOne 24 hour period of equipment running
R&RRemove and replace

If an abbreviation is not on the page, ask the company to spell it out. A scope you cannot read is a scope you cannot check.

Before you sign

  1. Match the rooms and measurements to your own walk through.
  2. Confirm the water source and date of loss are correct.
  3. Check that exclusions are listed and that you accept them.
  4. Ask how hidden damage and changes will be handled.
  5. Keep a signed copy and the photos taken at the start.

If the scope will go to an insurer, ask the company whether the format matches what the adjuster expects. Your insurer decides coverage, and a clear scope makes the review simpler.

Keeping the scope alive during the job

A scope is not a document you sign and forget. Ask for a short daily or visit note that lists readings, equipment moved and anything found behind walls. Compare it with the scope. If the work drifts, you can ask why while the crew is still on site.

At the end, ask for a final summary that matches the scope line by line, with photos of the finished state. That record helps with an insurer, a sale or a future problem in the same room.

Who to ask in Hollister about permit and rebuild questions

If the scope includes structural, electrical or plumbing repair, ask whether a permit applies. For addresses inside city limits, City of Hollister Development Services is the contact. For properties outside the city, the San Benito County Resource Management Agency handles building matters.

A scope should say who is responsible for permits, if any are needed. We do not state permit rules from memory, because they depend on the work.

Questions homeowners ask

What should a scope of work include?

Rooms, actions, quantities, equipment, days, exclusions and a way to approve changes.

Can I ask for changes to a scope?

Yes. Ask the company to put any change in writing before work begins.

Does a scope guarantee insurance approval?

No. The insurer decides coverage. The scope documents what is needed.

Who handles permits listed in a scope near Hollister?

Ask the company who pulls them. City of Hollister Development Services covers city addresses and the San Benito County Resource Management Agency covers unincorporated areas.
