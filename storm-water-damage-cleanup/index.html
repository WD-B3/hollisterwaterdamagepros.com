Storm water damage is an emergency when rain or wind has opened your home to the weather. Dispatch answers 24/7, even in the middle of a storm, and we confirm availability for your address on the call. Stay safe first, and do not go on a wet roof or into standing water near electrical items.

This page covers storm damage that enters through the building envelope: roofs, windows, doors, siding and vents. Rising water from rivers and runoff is a different case and is covered on flood damage cleanup.

Storm water: what to do first

  • Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings or active leaks.
  • Place buckets under drips and move contents away from wet walls.
  • Cover a broken window or damaged roof section with a tarp if it is safe to do so.
  • Photograph the damage from inside and, if it is safe, from outside.
  • Call dispatch for the interior water, and a roofer for the exterior repair.

How storm water gets in

Vents and chimneysFailed flashing or bootsCeilings and insulation below

Where storm damage and ceiling damage overlap

A storm often ends up as a ceiling problem, because roof water soaks insulation and drywall before it shows. If the leak is limited to one ceiling, our ceiling water damage repair page goes deeper on the repair steps. This page focuses on whole-event storm response, where several areas may be wet at once.

After a storm, a roofer or builder handles the exterior and we handle the interior. Keeping the two separate matters for your claim, because the interior water damage, drying logs and photographs are one set of records and the roof repair invoice is another.

How we dry a storm-damaged home

1

Assess every wet area

Meters and thermal imaging find water in attics, ceilings, walls and floors, including areas that look dry.

2

Remove saturated materials

Wet insulation and ruined drywall are removed so they stop feeding moisture to framing.

3

Dry the structure

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the building, as covered under structural drying.

4

Document

Photographs and drying logs support your insurance claim.

Check the forecast and what is mapped for your address

We are not going to state rainfall totals or past storm dates from memory. For current conditions and warnings, check the National Weather Service forecast for the Hollister area. For flood hazard mapping at your address, check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask the City of Hollister or San Benito County about local rules.

Highway corridors such as State Route 25 and State Route 156 can be affected by weather, so plan for possible delays if you are traveling. Dispatch handles the schedule, and we do not promise arrival times during storms.

What to have ready when you call

  • Where water entered, such as a roof, window or wall.
  • Which rooms and ceilings are wet.
  • Whether the electricity is on in wet areas.
  • Whether a roofer or builder has been called.
  • Photographs of any exterior damage.

If more rain is expected, ask a roofer about a temporary cover. We can dry the inside, but drying does not hold until the opening is closed.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

  • Climbing onto a wet roof.
  • Leaving wet insulation in the attic because the ceiling looks dry.
  • Repainting stained ceilings before the area has been checked with a meter.

Check the attic and exterior walls

Storm water often runs along rafters and down inside walls before it shows on a ceiling. A look at the attic insulation, the underside of the roof deck and the base of exterior walls can reveal where water entered and how far it traveled.

What affects storm damage cleanup cost

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Where water entered. Roof entry usually wets attic and ceiling, window entry wets walls and floors.
  • Amount of water. Longer storms soak more insulation and drywall.
  • Materials. Insulation, drywall, flooring and finishes each have their own steps.
  • Drying days. Equipment time depends on readings.
  • Rebuild scope. Interior repair follows the roofer or builder.

Insurance in general terms: wind and rain damage from a storm is often covered, while rising flood water usually is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer. Want your real number? Call (831) 271-0459.

Safety first and the standard we reference

We reference ANSI/IICRC S500 for categorizing storm water and setting drying goals. It is a published standard we cite, not a certification claim.

  • Never climb onto a wet roof.
  • Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings.
  • Keep power off where water entered.

Questions about Storm Water Damage in Hollister

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Hollister?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, and we confirm availability for your address on the call, though storms can affect scheduling.

Is storm damage covered by insurance?

Often for wind and rain damage, but flood damage usually needs separate coverage. Ask your insurer.

Should I tarp the roof myself?

Only if it is safe and dry. A roofer should handle temporary roof repairs, and you should not climb a wet roof.

Do you fix the roof?

No. We handle the interior water damage. A roofer repairs the roof.
