Water Damage Insurance Claim: Documentation and Steps in Hollister, CA

What to photograph, what to save and how to keep a clean paper trail from the first hour.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

A water damage claim goes more smoothly when the paperwork is organized from the first hour. Photos, receipts, call notes and drying records tell the story that the adjuster cannot see. This guide covers the steps homeowners usually take, written as general information and not as legal or insurance advice.

The first hour

  1. Make the space safe. Stop the water source and stay away from electrical hazards.
  2. Take wide photos of each room, then close-ups of the damage, the source and the water line.
  3. Record a short video walking through the damage and narrating what you see.
  4. Call your insurer or agent to report the loss and ask for a claim number.
  5. Call a restoration company so drying can begin, and tell them you will file a claim.

Keep a log and keep receipts

Start a simple log with dates, times, names and what was said every time you speak to your insurer, a plumber or a contractor. Keep copies of every letter and email. Save receipts for emergency repairs, temporary housing, meals and anything else you pay for because of the loss.

Many policies require that you protect the property from more damage, and receipts for reasonable protective steps are part of that. If you are not sure whether something qualifies, ask the adjuster before you spend a large amount.

What a restoration company adds

A restoration crew records moisture readings, equipment placement and the scope of work, which gives the adjuster objective data. Drying logs show when materials reached their dry target, and photographs show what was removed and why. We keep those for each job and explain the scope and price before work starts.

We do not negotiate with insurers on your behalf and we do not promise a claim outcome. What we do is document what we did so you have it. For the drying process itself, see structural drying, and for a burst line, see burst pipe water damage.

If the claim is slow or denied

Ask your insurer to explain in writing. If you disagree, you can ask the California Department of Insurance for help, and an attorney or public adjuster can advise on disputes. Keep every document, because the record you built in the first week matters most later.

Owners in Tres Pinos and San Juan Bautista follow the same state rules as Hollister owners, though rural properties sometimes have additional questions about wells and septic systems.

What the adjuster will want to see

Adjusters work from evidence. They look at the source of the water, the extent of the damage, the materials affected and whether you acted promptly. A clear set of photographs, a short written timeline and the drying records from your restoration company cover most of what they ask for.

  • A timeline of when you discovered the loss and what you did.
  • Photographs and video of the damage before cleanup.
  • The invoice or estimate from the plumber who stopped the source.
  • Drying logs, equipment lists and the scope of work from the restoration crew.
  • An inventory of damaged contents with approximate values.

Most of these are easy to avoid when you know them in advance. The trick is to remember them while stressed, which is why many people keep a short checklist on a phone or on the fridge.

Working with your contractor and your insurer together

Your insurer may have its own approved process, such as asking for an estimate or choosing a mitigation vendor. You can generally protect your home immediately, but ask your insurer what it requires so you do not duplicate work. Dispatch can start the drying plan while you make that call.

Keeping a claim file

Create one folder, paper or digital, for the claim. Put in it the claim number, the adjuster's name and contact details, every estimate and invoice, and a running log of calls. When a detail comes up weeks later, the file answers it in seconds, and it shows the insurer that the loss was handled carefully.

If you disagree with an adjuster's figure, ask for the reasoning in writing and compare it with your restoration scope line by line. Many differences come down to a missing room or a different assumption about days of drying, and both can be explained with records.

State guidance worth knowing

The California Department of Insurance advises keeping a log of all phone calls and correspondence, and copies of every letter sent to or received from your insurer. It also advises keeping receipts for temporary repairs and additional living expenses, and not discarding damaged items until the adjuster says to.

The same guide says a denial must be in writing with specific reasons and must say that the Department can review the matter. The Department's consumer hotline is 1-800-927-4357.

Source type: California Department of Insurance consumer guide.

Questions homeowners ask

What should I photograph after water damage?

The source, the water line on walls, every affected room and every damaged item. Take wide shots and close-ups, and record a video as well.

Should I wait for the adjuster before drying?

No. Protecting the property from more damage is usually expected. Document first, then start extraction and drying, and keep records.

Can you talk to my insurance company for me?

We provide documentation such as moisture logs and photographs. You manage the claim with your insurer.

Who can help if my Hollister claim is denied?

The California Department of Insurance can review a denial, and its guide lists a consumer hotline. You can also consult an attorney or public adjuster.
