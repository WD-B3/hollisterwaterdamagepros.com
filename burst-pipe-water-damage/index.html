Emergency Burst Pipe Water Damage in Hollister, CA

A pressurized supply line is pushing water through your home. Shut off the main, then call dispatch for cleanup and drying.

Call (831) 271-0459

A burst pipe is an emergency because supply lines run under pressure and keep pushing water until the valve is closed. Dispatch answers 24/7, and the first thing to do is find and close your main water shutoff. It is usually where the water line enters the house, at a meter box, or near the water heater.

Once the water is off, turn off power to wet areas only if the breaker panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain remaining pressure, move valuables out of the wet zone, and call us. A plumber will repair the pipe, and we handle the water damage that the leak left behind.

Burst pipe: what to do first

  1. Close the main water valve and confirm the flow has stopped.
  2. Keep people and pets out of standing water, and avoid it entirely near outlets or appliances.
  3. Photograph the damage and the broken pipe before anything is moved.
  4. Lift rugs, furniture and electronics away from wet floors and walls.
  5. Call the 24/7 line so extraction and drying can begin the same day when a crew is available.

Why burst pipes cause so much damage

A supply line can leak more water in an hour than a dripping fixture does in a month, and a line inside a wall or ceiling sends water down through every layer below it. Drywall wicks moisture upward from a wet baseboard, and insulation inside the cavity holds water against framing. That is why a burst pipe that looks like one wet spot often turns out to be several wet rooms.

Pipes fail for ordinary reasons. Age and corrosion weaken metal lines, fittings loosen, pressure spikes strain old joints, and pipes in unconditioned spaces can split when the temperature drops. If the break came from cold weather, the symptoms and steps are close to those on our frozen pipe water damage page.

How we handle the water after the pipe is stopped

Extraction

Standing water is pumped and extracted so it stops soaking into materials. See water extraction for how that step works.

Moisture mapping

Meters and thermal imaging show wet drywall, insulation and subfloor, including areas that look dry.

Selective removal

Wet insulation and badly saturated drywall near the break may be cut out so the cavity can dry.

Controlled drying

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, with readings checked until materials reach dry standards.

Safety first around wet electrical areas

If water has reached outlets, light fixtures, an electrical panel or an appliance, stay out of the room. Cut power only if you can do so from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any sign of sparks, burning smells or gas before calling the restoration line.

Pipes, ground movement and older Hollister homes

Hollister has an unusual local factor. The Calaveras Fault runs through town and creeps slowly, and U.S. Geological Survey creep meters in downtown Hollister have recorded movement of roughly 5 millimeters per year. Whether ground movement ever contributes to a particular pipe failure is a question for a licensed plumber, so ask yours to inspect the whole line rather than only the broken section.

Older homes near Fourth Street, Locust Street and the historic downtown often have galvanized or early copper lines that have been in service for decades. If one section failed, the plumber may recommend checking nearby joints. Meanwhile, we focus on getting the structure dry and documenting the loss for your claim.

Mistakes that make a burst pipe loss worse

  • Waiting to close the main valve while looking for a mop. Water keeps flowing until the valve is shut.
  • Running ceiling fans or vacuums in wet rooms.
  • Painting or patching a wet wall before it is dry.
  • Throwing away damaged materials before photographing them.
  • Assuming a dry-looking wall is dry. Cavities can stay wet for days.

None of these are unusual. Pipe failures happen at inconvenient moments, and the right order of steps is not obvious until you have done it once. That is why dispatch walks callers through the first steps on the phone.

What affects the price of burst pipe cleanup

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Water category. A clean supply line is category 1, but water that sat in walls for days can degrade.
  • Rooms and square footage. Water that traveled through a ceiling into the room below doubles the affected area.
  • Materials. Drywall, insulation, flooring and cabinets each respond differently to soaking.
  • Drying days. Equipment count and drying duration depend on moisture readings.
  • Rebuild. Removed drywall and flooring must be replaced after the structure is dry.

For insurance, sudden and accidental pipe bursts are often covered, while damage from a long slow leak often is not. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photographs, the plumber invoice and our written scope together. Want your real number? Call (831) 271-0459.

Safety first and the standard we reference

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard treats supply line water as clean water at first, and it warns that clean water becomes worse the longer it sits. That is why we move fast on burst lines.

  • Close the main valve before anything else.
  • Keep power off in rooms where water is running down walls.
  • Stay out from under a bulging ceiling.

Questions about Burst Pipe Water Damage in Hollister

Do you handle burst pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Hollister?

Yes. Dispatch runs 24/7 and we confirm availability for your address on the call. Shut off the main valve first, then call.

Do I call a plumber or a restoration company first?

Do both. A plumber repairs the pipe, and a restoration crew removes the water and dries the structure. Closing the main valve yourself buys time for both calls.

How do I know if water is inside the walls?

Look for bubbling paint, warm or soft drywall, musty smells, or a stain that keeps growing. Moisture meters and thermal imaging confirm what is hidden.

Will insurance pay for a burst pipe?

Sudden bursts are often covered, but policies differ. Ask your insurer what applies to your home and document everything before cleanup starts.
