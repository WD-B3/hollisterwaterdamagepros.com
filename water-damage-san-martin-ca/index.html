Water damage restoration in San Martin, CA, ZIP 95046, is a 24/7 service dispatched from Hollister, roughly 24 miles by road, by way of State Route 25 and US 101. San Martin is a community of about 6,400 residents in southern Santa Clara County, just over the county line from San Benito County and about 6.8 miles north of Gilroy.

A county line that changes who you call

Because San Martin is in Santa Clara County, building and permit questions go to Santa Clara County, not to Hollister or San Benito County. That affects who approves a repair, who inspects it and where the records live. We recommend asking the county office before rebuild work starts, and keeping your drying logs with the permit file.

Dispatch is the same 24/7 line, but we ask for cross streets and any gate or private-road details, because the drive crosses county lines and some parcels sit well back from the road.

Wells, septic and what a backup means here

The Santa Clara County Local Agency Formation Commission notes that San Martin is served mostly by individual and shared wells and that the large majority of the community has no sewer service. Wastewater is generally handled by individual septic systems. A backup from a septic line is contaminated water, and our sewage backup cleanup page explains the removal and disinfection steps.

A septic technician or plumber identifies the source and fixes the line, but cleanup does not wait for that diagnosis. Porous materials that touched the water, such as carpet and pad, are often removed.

Before we arrive in San Martin

Close the supply

Shut the main valve or the well pressure system, and close the valve at the leaking fixture.

Cut power with care

Turn off power to wet areas only from a dry spot. Otherwise call the electric utility or 911.

Move valuables

Lift furniture, tack and stored items off wet floors, and open cabinets and closet doors.

Stay out of dirty water

Keep children and pets away from septic, drain or outdoor water.

Seasons, storms and ranch properties

San Martin has a dry summer and a wet winter, and heavy rain can find its way into barns, garages and living spaces on rural parcels. Roofs on outbuildings and older homes are the usual entry points. Our flood damage cleanup page covers larger events, and storm water damage covers roof and window leaks. Check the FEMA flood map for your parcel if you are unsure of your risk.

Properties here often include detached garages, workshops and guest units. Each building with plumbing can have its own leak, so tell dispatch about all of them. Wet floors in older rural homes may sit over a crawl space, which our crawl space water damage page explains.

What dispatch asks a San Martin caller

Because the drive crosses a county line and many parcels sit back from the road, dispatch asks a few extra questions. Which road or lane are you on, and is there a gate or a code? Is the water from the house, a barn, a shop or a guest unit? Is a pressure tank or well pump involved? Those answers let the crew bring the right equipment and find the property without calling you back.

If power is out because of a storm, say so. Drying equipment needs electricity, and the crew may need to plan for a generator or wait until power returns before the drying phase begins. Extraction and removal of ruined materials can still start.
