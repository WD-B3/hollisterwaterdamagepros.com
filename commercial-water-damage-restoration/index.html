Commercial water damage is an emergency because every hour of water also costs revenue, stock and customer trust. Dispatch answers 24/7, including nights and weekends. If your shop, office, restaurant or rental building has water on the floor, close the main or building shutoff, protect inventory and records, and call.

We handle water damage restoration for small and mid-size commercial properties and multi-unit buildings. For very large industrial losses, ask us on the call whether a project fits our scope. We do not promise a response time, and we confirm availability for your address on the call.

Commercial water emergency: first steps

  1. Close the building or unit shutoff valve to stop the supply.
  2. Cut power only from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical hazard.
  3. Move inventory, records and electronics off wet floors and away from ceiling drips.
  4. Photograph the damage and make a list of affected stock and equipment.
  5. Call dispatch and your property manager or landlord if you lease.

Why business losses are different

A business cannot always close for a week. Commercial spaces have larger floor areas, more finishes such as ceiling tile and commercial carpet, and tenants, landlords and insurers who all want documentation. The plan must also fit how you operate, which may mean working in phases or after hours.

If your property is a rental, the lease decides who calls the insurer and who pays for contents, so involve your landlord early. For a ceiling that has collapsed or is stained, see ceiling water damage repair. If the cause is a burst line, burst pipe water damage explains the sequence.

What to have ready when you call

  • The type of business or building and the approximate square footage.
  • The source of the water and whether it is stopped.
  • Which areas hold inventory, records or equipment.
  • Whether you own or lease the space.
  • Your insurer or property manager contact.

If you are a tenant, ask your landlord who handles the building side and who handles contents. A clear answer before work starts saves arguments later.

Keeping a business running during a water loss

Ask whether the crew can work in zones so part of the space stays open. Tell dispatch about inventory, records and equipment that cannot get wet, and list the hours when work is least disruptive. A clear plan helps staff and customers know what to expect.

What affects commercial cleanup cost

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Area and layout. Larger floors and open spaces need more equipment.
  • Materials. Ceiling tile, carpet tile, drywall and flooring have different steps.
  • Water category. Clean, gray and contaminated water are handled differently.
  • Business needs. After-hours or phased work can change the plan and schedule.
  • Rebuild scope. Tenant improvements and finishes may need replacing afterward.

Business property policies differ, so check yours or ask your insurer about coverage for the building, contents and interruption of business. Want your real number? Call (831) 271-0459.

Safety first and the standard we reference

For commercial work we reference ANSI/IICRC S500 for water categories and drying, and we leave regulatory and lease questions to the right professional.

  • Keep customers and staff out of wet areas.
  • Turn off power to wet equipment only from a dry spot.
  • Photograph inventory and records before moving them.
