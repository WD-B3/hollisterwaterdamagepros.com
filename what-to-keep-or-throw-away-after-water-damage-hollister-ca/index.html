What to Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in Hollister, CA

A practical sorting guide by water type and material, so you do not toss what can be saved or keep what cannot.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

After water damage, the hardest job is deciding what to keep. Throw away too much and you lose things that could have been saved. Keep too much and you carry moisture, odor and contamination into the rebuild. Two questions decide most cases: what kind of water touched the item, and what is the item made of.

Start with the water type

Water typeExamplesGeneral rule for belongings
Clean (category 1)Burst supply line, broken water heater, rain through a roofMost hard items are saved. Porous items dry quickly or are discarded if left wet
Gray (category 2)Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeClean and disinfect hard items. Many porous items are discarded
Black (category 3)Sewage, flood water, standing water from outdoorsDiscard porous items. Clean and disinfect hard items only if sealed

If you are not sure which category applies, treat the water as the more contaminated one for decisions about food, bedding and children's items. The category is the first thing a restoration crew checks, and it changes the plan.

Sorting by material

  • Hard, non-porous items such as glass, metal, hard plastic and sealed tile can usually be cleaned and disinfected.
  • Solid wood furniture can often be dried and saved if the water was clean and it did not sit for long.
  • Particleboard and laminate furniture usually swells and does not recover.
  • Upholstered furniture, mattresses and pillows that touched contaminated water are usually discarded.
  • Carpet and pad that touched anything but clean water, or that stayed wet, are usually discarded.

Food, medicine and items that go in the mouth

Do not eat food that touched flood water or sewage, and discard sealed containers if the seal is compromised. Ask a pharmacist about medicine that was wet. Baby bottles, pacifiers and toys that touched contaminated water should be discarded. When in doubt, throw it out, and photograph first.

Clothing, textiles and paper

Clothing and linens that touched clean water can usually be laundered, and gray water items can be washed hot with disinfectant. Items from sewage or flood water are often discarded. Paper, books and cardboard that got wet absorb water and mold if left, so freeze valuable ones for later treatment and discard the rest.

Document before you discard

Take photographs of items before you throw them out, and keep a list with approximate age and value. Do not discard large items such as furniture or appliances until your insurer says it is fine, unless they pose a health risk. A simple notebook list is enough.

What a restoration company handles

A crew can pack out contents, store them, clean them off site and return them after drying, which protects items from the construction area. Contents work is separate from structural drying, which is covered on our structural drying page. For standing water in a room, see water extraction.

Neighbors in Tres Pinos and San Juan Bautista follow the same sorting logic. The only extra consideration for rural properties is whether the water came from a well system or septic tank, which changes the water type.

Quick sorting checklist

  1. Make the area safe and stop the water.
  2. Identify the water type.
  3. Photograph everything before moving it.
  4. Move salvageable hard items to a dry area and let them air out.
  5. Bag and label items you plan to discard, and keep the list.
  6. Ask your insurer before hauling large items away.

Room-by-room sorting notes

  • Kitchen: discard open food, wet cardboard packaging and anything porous that sat in the water. Wash hard dishes and cookware in hot soapy water, then sanitize.
  • Bedrooms: mattresses and box springs that soaked up water rarely dry fully inside. Solid bed frames can often be cleaned and dried.
  • Living areas: wet books, cushions and rugs are the usual losses. Hard-sided electronics need a technician before power.
  • Garage: stored cardboard boxes absorb water and hold it. Move contents to a dry area and open every box.

Work in one room at a time. Finishing a single room gives you a clear sense of progress and a pile you can photograph and list, which is easier than sorting the whole house at once.

How to sanitize hard items safely

Wash with hot water and a household detergent first, since disinfectant works poorly on dirt. Then apply a disinfectant that is labeled for the surface, follow the label for contact time, and rinse items that touch food. Never mix cleaning products, and open windows while you work.

Items that cannot be washed, such as stuffed toys that touched contaminated water, are usually discarded. If an item has real sentimental value, ask a cleaning professional what is possible before you decide.

When to bring in help with contents

If a room holds a lot of belongings, a pack-out service removes them, cleans them elsewhere and returns them. That also clears the room so the crew can dry the floor and walls without working around furniture. It is worth asking about when most of a room is affected.

Guidance to follow before you haul anything away

The California Department of Insurance advises homeowners not to discard damaged items until the adjuster says to, and to keep receipts for temporary repairs and additional living expenses. For a Hollister household, the practical version is to photograph and list items first, then ask the insurer before large disposal.

The same guide recommends listing damaged items with brand names and model numbers and taking photos. That list is useful for your claim and for your own records.

Source type: California Department of Insurance consumer guide.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I keep upholstered furniture after water damage?

If the water was clean and the furniture was dried quickly, sometimes. If it touched gray or contaminated water, it is usually discarded.

What about electronics?

Do not plug in wet electronics. A qualified repair shop can assess them, and we cover the basics on our electronics guide.

Should I wait for the adjuster before throwing things away?

Photograph and list items first, and ask your insurer before discarding large items unless they are a health hazard.

Do I have to ask my Hollister insurer before hauling a wet couch to the curb?

The California Department of Insurance advises waiting until the adjuster says to discard damaged items, so ask first and photograph the item.
