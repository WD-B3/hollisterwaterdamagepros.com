Water Damage Cleanup in the Downtown Hollister Historic District, CA

Water removal and drying for downtown Hollister businesses, offices and mixed-use buildings.

Call (831) 271-0459

Water damage cleanup in the Downtown Hollister Historic District starts with a different set of questions than a house does. Who owns the building, who rents which space, what stock or records are wet, and when does the business need to reopen? We ask those on the first call, then plan extraction and drying around the answers.

The district

The National Register listing, added on August 14, 1992, describes the Downtown Hollister Historic District as roughly bounded by Fourth, East, South and Monterey Streets and covering about 22 acres. The 1992 record counted 53 contributing buildings, 29 non-contributing buildings and 10 empty parcels. Contributing buildings include a Masonic Temple of about 1908 at 355 San Benito Street, a Carnegie library and a former post office at 135 5th Street, built around 1935.

The listed styles are Italianate, Mission or Spanish Revival and Spanish Colonial Revival. Those details tell us the district is a mix of civic, commercial and older masonry buildings. We do not know the plumbing or roofing of any particular building and will not assume it.

Immediate steps for a shop or office

  • Stop the source if you can reach the valve safely, and tell the building owner.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics off wet floors only if it is safe to do so.
  • Keep customers and staff out of wet areas and away from electrical equipment.
  • Photograph the damage and the high-water marks before cleanup.
  • Call the insurer and the property manager, then call us to set a plan.

Drying older masonry and mixed-use buildings

Masonry, plaster and old wood framing can all hold moisture, and a surface that looks dry can still read wet. The crew uses meters to map the damage, places dehumidifiers and air movers, and checks readings until the building reads dry. See structural drying for how those targets are set.

Records and approvals

The City of Hollister publishes inventories for this district, and Chapter 15.16 of the municipal code covers historic resources. Before changing exterior features or original materials, ask City of Hollister Development Services. Keep drying logs, photographs and the scope for your insurer and your landlord.

Flood zone note

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified for this page. To check a specific address, use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center, and ask the City of Hollister or San Benito County about local drainage rules.

Questions about water damage in Downtown Hollister Historic District

Do you handle businesses in downtown Hollister?

Yes. Call and describe the space, and dispatch will ask about the type of business, the source of the water and what is wet.

Who do I call first, the landlord or the restoration company?

Tell your landlord or property manager promptly, since they often control the building side. You can call us at the same time to start a plan.

Can drying be done without closing my shop?

Sometimes work can be zoned so part of the space stays open. It depends on how far the water spread and where equipment must go.

Do historic building rules affect water damage repair?

They may affect repairs to original features. Ask City of Hollister Development Services before you change original materials.

What if the leak started in the shop next door?

Tell both owners and photograph the damage. We can dry your space, and your insurer and the other party sort out responsibility.
