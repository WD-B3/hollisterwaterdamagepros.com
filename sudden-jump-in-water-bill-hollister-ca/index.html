Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill? How to Check for a Hidden Leak in Hollister

A higher bill is often the first sign. Here is a simple way to find out whether water is going somewhere it should not.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

A hidden leak rarely announces itself. Often the first sign is a bill that is higher than usual with no change in how you use water. Before you assume the meter is wrong, a few simple checks can tell you whether water is escaping inside your home or yard.

The meter test

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water.
  2. Find your water meter and note the reading, or watch the small leak indicator.
  3. Wait an hour or two without using water.
  4. Check again. If the number moved or the indicator is spinning, water is flowing somewhere.

This works because with everything off, the meter should be still. If it is not, there is a leak between the meter and your fixtures. It could be a toilet, an irrigation line, a slab line or a pipe in a wall.

If the meter test shows a leak but you cannot see it, a plumber or leak detection company can locate it with tools. Locating a leak is plumbing work, and drying the damage afterward is restoration work.

What to do next

  1. Shut off the main valve if water is flowing and you cannot find the source.
  2. Call a plumber to find and fix the leak.
  3. Photograph any damage you can see.
  4. Ask your water utility about your bill and whether any adjustment policy applies.
  5. Call a restoration company if floors, walls or ceilings are wet.

A slab leak is one of the harder cases, because water travels under the floor. See slab leak water damage for what damage looks like and how it is dried. For a known pipe failure inside a wall, burst pipe water damage covers the first steps.

Reading the bill itself

Compare this month to the same month last year, since use changes with season and irrigation. A jump of a few percent may be weather. A sudden doubling with no change in habits points to a leak or a running fixture. Look at the dates of the billing period too, because a longer period can raise the total.

Signs of a leak you can see or hear

  • A damp or warm spot on the floor.
  • The sound of water running when nothing is on.
  • A musty smell in a closet or cabinet.
  • Low water pressure that was normal before.
  • Unusually green or soggy patches of lawn.

Any one of these combined with a meter that moves is a strong clue. Shut off the water at the main valve while you wait for the plumber, and note where the valve is so everyone in the home can find it.

Keep the evidence

Save the bill, note the meter readings with dates and keep the plumber's invoice. Some utilities offer an adjustment if a leak is repaired promptly, and records show what happened. Ask your utility what they need before you request one.

Outdoor leaks that look like indoor ones

Not every leak is in the house. A broken irrigation line, a leaking hose bib or a cracked line between the meter and the house can raise your bill without a single wet floor. Look for soft ground, unusually green grass or water pooling near the foundation. Those spots can also send water toward the slab or crawl space over time.

If water is entering the home from outside, tell your restoration crew and your plumber, since both parts of the problem need to be fixed. Drying a wet floor does not help if the line keeps leaking.

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

Turn everything off and watch the meter. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere.

Can a toilet cause a big bill?

Yes. A toilet that runs or leaks into the bowl can waste a lot of water over a month.

Who fixes a hidden leak?

A plumber locates and repairs the leak. A restoration company dries the damage.
