Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Hollister, CA?

It depends on the work, and on whether you are inside the city or not. Here is how to find out.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

After water damage, there are two phases. The first is drying and removal, and the second is repair and rebuild. Permit questions mostly arise in the second phase, but some begin earlier, so it helps to ask before anyone starts cutting.

Which office answers the question

Where the property isWho to ask
Inside Hollister city limitsCity of Hollister Development Services
Unincorporated San Benito CountySan Benito County Resource Management Agency
Inside San Juan Bautista city limitsCity of San Juan Bautista
Santa Clara County (San Martin)Santa Clara County planning and building

If you are not sure which jurisdiction applies, ask the nearest office or check the county parcel lookup. A wrong guess can mean a call to the wrong place, and no one wants to waste time during a repair.

Work that commonly raises the question

  • Replacing structural framing, such as studs, joists or subfloor.
  • Electrical repair or rewiring in a wet area.
  • Plumbing repair that changes the layout.
  • Replacing a water heater.
  • Roof repair after storm damage.

Cosmetic work such as paint and carpet replacement often does not raise the question, but whether a permit applies is up to the local office, not to us. We do not state permit rules from memory.

How permits fit with mitigation

Emergency mitigation, such as extracting water and drying, is generally handled separately from rebuild. Ask the building office whether any emergency work needs notice. Keep the drying logs, because inspectors and insurers sometimes ask for them. See water extraction and structural drying for what those logs contain.

Questions to ask the office

  1. Does my repair need a permit, and what type?
  2. Who can apply, the owner or a contractor?
  3. Which inspections are required, and when?
  4. What documents should I bring?
  5. How long does review typically take?

Write down the name of the person you speak to, the date and what they said. If the repair is in Tres Pinos or San Juan Bautista, confirm which jurisdiction applies first, since the answer changes who you call.

What to bring when you call or visit

  • Your address and parcel number.
  • A short description of what was damaged and what repair you plan.
  • Photographs of the damage.
  • The scope of work or contractor estimate, if you have one.
  • The name and license information of the contractor, if hired.

Having these ready makes the conversation shorter and the answer clearer. Staff can tell you whether a permit applies and which documents you need to submit.

Keeping your repair on schedule

Permits and inspections add steps, so ask about timing early. If drying is finished and rebuild is waiting on paperwork, ask the office whether partial work such as drying and removal can proceed while review is underway. The answer depends on the office and the work.

Keep every permit, inspection sign-off and receipt in one folder. These papers matter for insurance and for a future sale, since a buyer may ask whether past repairs were permitted and finished.

Working with a contractor on permits

If you hire a contractor for repair, ask in writing who is responsible for permits and inspections. A clear answer prevents the situation where each side assumes the other handled it. Ask to see the permit card posted at the job when one applies.

Check a contractor's license status through the Contractors State License Board before you hire, and keep a copy of the contract. For a small job, the board's published rules on license thresholds tell you when a license is required, and we do not restate them from memory.

If you are doing the repair yourself, ask the building office about owner builder rules, because they come with responsibilities of their own.

Where to start in San Benito County

City of Hollister Development Services handles building, planning and related permits for properties inside the city. The San Benito County Resource Management Agency handles building and code enforcement for unincorporated areas, which includes many rural parcels around Tres Pinos.

San Juan Bautista has its own city government with separate staff. We cannot tell you whether a specific repair needs a permit, and we do not guess. Call the office that covers your parcel and ask before work starts.

Source type: City of Hollister, San Benito County and City of San Juan Bautista websites.

Questions homeowners ask

Does drying a wet house need a permit?

Emergency drying generally does not raise the same questions as rebuild, but ask your local office to be sure.

Who pulls the permit?

It depends on the work and the office. Ask the building department who may apply.

Can I repair water damage myself?

Owners can do some work, but electrical, plumbing and structural work may have rules. Ask the office first.

Which office do I call if my address is outside Hollister city limits?

The San Benito County Resource Management Agency handles unincorporated parcels. San Juan Bautista has its own city office.
