How older buildings change the drying plan

San Juan Bautista grew around Mission San Juan Bautista, founded in 1797, and the area around the mission and 3rd Street still holds some of the oldest buildings in San Benito County. Older structures often have plaster over lath, original wood floors, raised foundations and plumbing that has been repaired in pieces. Each of those reacts differently to water.

Plaster can hold moisture behind a dry-looking face, and old wood floors can cup or stain. A crew measures first and removes only what readings say cannot dry. For hardwood in particular, see our hardwood floor water damage page.

Before we arrive in San Juan Bautista

Shut off the supply

Find the main valve, and in older buildings check for a second shutoff near the meter or crawl space access.

Protect the structure

Move rugs, artwork and furniture off wet floors, and lay towels along wet walls, but do not scrub plaster.

Power safely

Cut power to wet rooms only from a dry spot at the panel, and avoid old fuse boxes if you are unsure.

Avoid contaminated water

Stay out of water that came from a drain, toilet or outdoors until it is assessed.

Seasonal patterns along the SR 156 corridor

The wet season brings roof and window leaks, and the dry summer can hide plumbing leaks that only show when irrigation or heavy use resumes. The San Andreas Fault runs close to San Juan Bautista, and any shaking can loosen fittings or supply lines, so a plumber should check after a notable earthquake. What a crew checks in a building like yours

In an older San Juan Bautista building, the crew looks at the foundation type first. A raised floor over a vented crawl space dries differently from a slab, and old subfloor boards can hide moisture long after the surface feels dry. The crew then checks walls, baseboards and door casings with a meter, because water often travels along framing and shows up far from the source.

Original wood trim and plaster are worth saving when they can be, so removal is limited to what readings say cannot dry. Newer homes on the edges of town are more likely to have drywall and engineered flooring, and those follow the standard plan on our wall and drywall and carpet pages.

Permits and the city
