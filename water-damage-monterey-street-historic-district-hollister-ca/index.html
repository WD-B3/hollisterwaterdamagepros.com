Water Damage Restoration in the Monterey Street Historic District, Hollister, CA

Careful water removal and drying for older Hollister homes with original wood floors, trim and plaster.

Call (831) 271-0459

Water damage restoration in the Monterey Street Historic District, Hollister, CA, is about protecting character as well as drying a structure. The district is on the National Register of Historic Places, and owners of older buildings often want to keep original floors, trim and wall finishes. A good plan removes the water quickly and takes out only what readings say cannot dry.

About the district

The National Park Service lists the Monterey Street Historic District, added to the Register on January 7, 1993. Its record describes Monterey Street and intersecting streets between 5th Street and B Street, with 188 contributing buildings. Periods of significance run from 1875 to 1949, and the architectural styles named include Bungalow and Craftsman and Queen Anne.

The listing does not tell us how many buildings are homes today, so we do not claim a number. We also do not claim that any building here has a particular plumbing material or foundation. We look at each one when we arrive.

Why older buildings need a measured approach

  • Solid wood floors can cup, crown or stain, and sanding too early can cause damage that lasts.
  • Plaster and older wall finishes may hold moisture behind a dry-looking surface.
  • Raised floors over a crawl space dry differently from a slab, and need access for equipment.
  • Original trim and doors may be worth saving, so removal is kept to the minimum that readings support.

Our hardwood floor water damage page explains how boards react to water, and our crawl space water damage page covers moisture under a raised floor. For walls and ceilings, see wall and drywall water damage and ceiling water damage repair.

What to do first

1

Stop the water

Close the main valve or the fixture valve, and call a plumber if the line is still running.

2

Protect floors and finishes

Lift rugs and furniture, and wipe standing water from wood without scrubbing.

3

Do not heat or sand

Avoid heat guns and sanding. They can crack boards and trap moisture.

4

Photograph and call

Document the damage, then call so the plan can be set before more materials soak.

Repairs and approvals in a historic district

The City of Hollister keeps a historic resources page and refers to Chapter 15.16 of the municipal code for historic resources. We cannot say whether a particular repair needs city review. Before replacing original materials or altering a historic building, ask City of Hollister Development Services what applies, and keep your drying records with the file.

Insurance and records

Coverage for older buildings depends on the cause of the loss and your policy. Ask your insurer early whether the policy treats original materials differently. Photographs before work starts, moisture readings and a written scope all help describe what happened and what was done.

Questions about water damage in Monterey Street Historic District

Can original wood floors in an older Hollister home be saved after water damage?

Often, if they are dried promptly and the water was clean. Boards that have cupped or stained may need more time, and readings decide whether they can stay.

Does the Monterey Street Historic District have special rules for repairs?

The City of Hollister maintains historic resources information and cites Chapter 15.16 of its code. Ask Development Services whether your repair is affected before you begin.

Will you remove plaster or trim?

Only what readings show cannot dry, or what is contaminated. We aim to keep original materials where they can be dried safely.

Is every building on Monterey Street a home?

We do not know, and the National Park Service listing does not say. Call and tell us whether your building is residential or commercial.

What if the water came from a toilet or a sewer line?

That is contaminated water. Keep people and pets away, and call so the plan can include removal and cleaning of porous materials.
