Water Damage and Electronics: When to Unplug in Hollister, CA

Water and electricity do not mix. This guide covers what to unplug, what not to touch and who to call.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

When water reaches electrical items, the first question is safety, not rescue. Wet outlets, cords and panels can shock or start a fire, and electronics that look fine can fail after they dry. This guide explains when to unplug, when to stay out and what to do with wet devices.

Start with safety

Safety comes first

If water is standing near outlets, an electrical panel or appliances, do not enter. Cut power only if you can reach the panel from a completely dry spot. If you cannot, call the electric utility or 911. Never touch a person or device that may be energized.

When to unplug

  • If a device is wet and you can reach the plug from dry ground, unplug it.
  • If water is on the floor around the outlet, do not touch the plug.
  • If a power strip is wet, switch off the circuit at the panel first.
  • If you smell burning or see sparks, leave and call 911.

Once a device is unplugged, do not turn it on to see if it works. Moisture inside can cause a short, and a device that powers on may fail later. Let a qualified repair shop assess it.

Rice does not reliably dry electronics. Silica gel and open air are better, but the best choice is a professional assessment. Salt water and sewage are especially harmful, and corrosion can appear days later.

Outlets, panels and wiring after a wet event

If water got into walls, outlets or the electrical panel, have a licensed electrician inspect before power is restored. Wet insulation and drywall around wiring should be dried, and a crew can dry the cavity while the electrician checks the circuits. See wall and drywall water damage repair and structural drying for how that works.

For a larger flood event that reached outlets, flood damage cleanup explains the safety steps. Rural homes in Tres Pinos and San Juan Bautista may have outbuildings or well pumps with their own electrical equipment, so mention those to the electrician.

Claim notes

Photograph wet electronics and list brand, model and age before disposal. Keep receipts for any repair or replacement, and ask your insurer what documentation it needs. Many policies cover sudden water damage to electronics, but terms and limits differ.

Signs that electrical damage may be hiding

  • Outlets or switches that feel warm or look discolored.
  • Lights that flicker after a wet event.
  • A breaker that trips again as soon as it is reset.
  • A burning or fishy smell near a wall or panel.
  • Crackling or buzzing sounds from a wet area.

Any of these after a leak or flood means stop using the circuit and have an electrician check it. A tripped breaker is a warning, not a nuisance to reset until it holds.

Power tools, extension cords and equipment during cleanup

Cleanup itself can create electrical risk. Use equipment plugged into ground fault protected outlets, keep cords out of standing water and never use a household vacuum on wet floors. Professional crews use ground fault protection and place cords so they do not sit in water.

Gas, batteries and other hazards

If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility or 911. Lithium batteries in laptops, tools and e-bikes that were soaked can overheat later, so keep them on a hard surface outdoors or in a garage and away from flammable items until a professional assesses them.

After the water is out and the electrician has cleared the circuits, the drying process can proceed safely. That order, safety first and then drying, protects both people and equipment.

A short action plan

  1. Stay out of rooms with standing water and live electrical equipment.
  2. Shut off power at the panel only from a dry spot.
  3. Call the electric utility or 911 for hazards.
  4. Unplug what you can safely reach and photograph it.
  5. Have a licensed electrician inspect before restoring power.
  6. Then call dispatch to start drying.

Following this order keeps people safe first and protects the home second. Dry-out can start as soon as the electrical risk is handled, and the earlier that happens the less damage the wall cavities and floors suffer.

Who to ask in Hollister about electrical repairs

Building and permit questions inside city limits go to City of Hollister Development Services. For properties outside the city, the San Benito County Resource Management Agency handles building and code enforcement.

Whether an electrical repair after water damage needs a permit depends on the work, so ask the relevant office before an electrician or contractor starts. We do not state permit rules from memory.

Questions homeowners ask

Is it safe to turn on electronics that got wet?

No. Unplug them and let a qualified technician assess them before you power them on.

What about outlets that were wet?

Do not use them until a licensed electrician inspects them and the wall is dry.

Can I dry electronics in rice?

It is not reliable. Open air and silica gel are better, and a technician is best.

Does an electrical repair after water damage need a permit in Hollister?

It depends on the work. Ask City of Hollister Development Services if you are inside the city, or the San Benito County Resource Management Agency if you are outside.
