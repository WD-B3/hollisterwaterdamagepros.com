What to Ask a Water Damage Restoration Company Before You Hire in Hollister, CA

A practical list of questions to ask on the phone, and what a good answer sounds like.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

Water damage forces fast decisions, and the company you call first is often the one you hire. A few well-chosen questions help you compare plans and spot vague answers. These are the ones that matter most, with notes on what a good answer sounds like.

Licensing, insurance and standards

  • Is the company licensed and insured, and can you check that yourself?
  • Do the crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration?
  • Who will be on site, and are they employees or subcontractors?
  • Will there be a written agreement before work starts?

S500 is a published industry standard, and a good company can say whether it follows it. A simple answer to these questions is better than a long speech. Check licenses with the state contractors board before you sign.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and we do not offer a flat price without seeing the space. Exact prices depend on the water category, affected area, materials, drying days and rebuild scope, which is why a real number needs an inspection.

Equipment and monitoring

  • What equipment will you use, such as moisture meters, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers?
  • How will you decide when the structure is dry?
  • Will you keep a drying log with readings?
  • How many visits will there be?

Drying is measured, not guessed. A company that explains moisture mapping and daily readings is describing the process correctly, and you can read about it on our structural drying page. For standing water, see water extraction.

Documentation and insurance

Ask what records you will receive, such as photographs, moisture logs and an itemized scope. Ask what the company needs from you for your claim. Do not accept promises about what your insurer will approve, because only the insurer decides.

If you are in Tres Pinos or San Juan Bautista, ask about travel and scheduling, and expect an honest answer about when a crew can arrive. We do not promise arrival times.

Red flags on the phone

  • A hard sell that pushes you to sign before anyone has seen the space.
  • A refusal to explain the plan in plain language.
  • A promise of what your insurer will pay.
  • A claim that no equipment is needed because they will just fix it.
  • A request for full payment up front.

None of these on its own proves a company is dishonest, but together they are a good reason to call another one. Emergency work is stressful, and some companies count on that.

Questions about what happens after drying

Ask whether the company does the rebuild or hands it to another contractor, and how the scope is divided. A clear answer helps you plan, and it matters for permits, because rebuild work may need them. Ask who pulls the permit if one is needed, and who inspects what.

Also ask about warranties in plain terms. We do not offer any warranty claims on this site, and you should be wary of any company that promises that moisture will never return. Moisture returns when the source returns, which is a plumbing or drainage matter.

What a clear first call sounds like

A good first call ends with you knowing what happens next. Dispatch asks where the water came from, which rooms are wet and whether anyone is in danger. They tell you what to do while you wait, such as closing the main valve or staying out of rooms with standing water near outlets.

You should also hear how scope and price are handled. We explain scope and price before work starts, and a company that cannot tell you how its estimate works is not ready to handle a loss in your home. Use this call as a test, since the way a company handles it says a lot about how it handles the job.

A California licensing note

The Contractors State License Board reports that, effective January 1, 2025, Assembly Bill 2622 raised the threshold for work requiring a contractor's license from $500 to $1,000 in labor and materials. The exemption applies only if the unlicensed person hires no workers and the project needs no building permits.

For a Hollister water damage project, the practical step is to ask for a license number and check it with the state contractors board before you sign anything larger than minor work.

Questions homeowners ask

Should I get more than one quote?

If you have time, yes. Water damage is time-sensitive, so ask your questions quickly and do not let a slow comparison delay drying.

Is a lower price a better deal?

Not automatically. Compare the scope, equipment and days, not just the total.

Can I check a contractor license myself?

Yes. The California Contractors State License Board provides a license check, and it raised the licensing threshold to $1,000 effective January 1, 2025.

Do Hollister companies charge differently for nights and weekends?

Policies vary, so ask on the call. We explain scope and price before work starts.
