Flood water entering a home is an emergency, and it is a different kind of job from a broken pipe. Water from rain, runoff or groundwater has touched soil, yards and drains, which is why it is treated as contaminated. Dispatch answers 24/7, and we confirm availability for your address on the call.

Before you do anything else, stay safe. Do not walk through flood water that could be near outlets or appliances, and do not wade into deep water. Cut power only from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us. If the water is still rising, leave if it is not safe to stay.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is an emergency whenever it is inside living space, because moisture soaks into drywall, insulation and subfloor within hours. Contaminated water also carries bacteria that make porous materials unsafe to keep. Starting removal and cleaning early limits how much must be thrown away.

How flood damage cleanup works

1

Assess and protect

The crew checks safety, shuts off or isolates hazards, and plans which rooms are affected.

2

Extract and remove

Pumps and extraction units remove water, and soaked carpet, pad and wet drywall may be taken out.

3

Clean and sanitize

Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products suited to the water category.

4

Dry and monitor

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings confirm when the drying goal is met. See structural drying.

Flood safety checklist

Do not use household vacuums, fans or electronics in wet areas. Do not eat food that touched flood water. Wear boots and gloves if you must enter. Document the water line on walls with photographs before it is cleaned.

Flood insurance and your claim

Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies, and photograph the high-water line, damaged contents and any visible debris before cleanup. Never discard heavily damaged items until your insurer has had a chance to see them or approve disposal.

What to document before cleanup

  • The high-water line on walls, cabinets and furniture.
  • Standing water in each room, with a ruler or phone beside it for scale.
  • Damaged contents, with brand and approximate age.
  • The route water used to enter, such as a door, garage or foundation crack.
  • Any debris or silt left behind.

These records help your insurer and your contractor, and they take only a few minutes. Take them from a safe spot and never enter water near electrical equipment to get a photograph.

What affects flood cleanup cost

Cost factors we explain before work starts

  • Water category. Contaminated flood water adds safety, removal and disinfecting steps.
  • Depth and area. Water that reached walls and subfloor affects far more material.
  • Materials. Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall are often removed, and hard surfaces may be saved.
  • Drying days. Larger loss means more equipment and more days.
  • Rebuild scope. Removed materials must be replaced after drying, and permits may apply.

Safety first and the standard we reference

For flood water, ANSI/IICRC S500 describes the highest contamination category and what that means for porous materials. We follow that reasoning when we decide what is removed.

  • Stay out of flood water until power is confirmed off.
  • Wear boots and gloves if you must enter.
  • Never mix cleaning chemicals.

Questions about Flood Damage Cleanup in Hollister

Do you handle flood damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Hollister?

Yes. Dispatch is available 24/7 and we confirm availability for your address on the call. Stay safe, then call.

Is flood damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually not. Flood damage typically needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

How long does flood cleanup take?

Extraction and removal can happen in a day or two, and drying typically takes several days. Larger losses and rebuilds take longer.

Can flood-damaged items be saved?

Hard, non-porous items can often be cleaned. Porous items such as carpet pad, upholstered furniture and mattresses that touched flood water are usually discarded.
