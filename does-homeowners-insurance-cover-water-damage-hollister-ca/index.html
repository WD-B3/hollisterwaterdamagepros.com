Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Hollister, CA?

A plain guide to sudden versus gradual damage, flood exclusions and the questions to ask your insurer.

Call (831) 271-0459

By the Hollister Water Damage Pros team. Published and reviewed November 10, 2026.

The short answer is that it depends on the cause. Standard homeowners policies often cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a pipe that bursts or a water heater that fails. They often do not cover damage from slow leaks, neglect or flooding, and each policy has its own wording. We are a restoration company, not an insurer or a lawyer, so treat this as background to bring to your own insurer.

What a policy usually covers

Sudden and accidental events are the usual pattern for coverage. A supply line that splits overnight, a washing machine hose that bursts or rain that enters through a roof opened by a covered event are all examples insurers commonly treat as covered. The policy typically pays to repair the damage, and many also pay to dry the structure.

Even when the event is covered, you may owe a deductible, and some items have limits. Ask your insurer how your deductible applies to water losses and whether your policy has separate limits for certain items such as jewelry or electronics.

What a policy usually does not cover

SituationOften covered?Why it matters
Burst pipe or sudden appliance failureOften yesSudden and accidental
Slow leak over monthsOften noTreated as maintenance or neglect
Flood from rising water or runoffUsually noNeeds a separate flood policy
Sewer or drain backupOnly with an add-onOften an optional endorsement
Wear and tear of the plumbing itselfOften noThe pipe repair may not be covered even if the damage is

Some policies cover the damage from a leak but not the cost to find and repair the broken pipe itself. That distinction surprises many people, so ask your insurer how it treats access and repair costs. If you suspect a slow leak, tell your insurer early and keep records.

Steps that protect your claim

  1. Stop the water if you safely can, and take reasonable steps to prevent more damage.
  2. Photograph and video the damage before moving anything.
  3. Call your insurer to report the loss and ask what they need.
  4. Keep receipts for temporary repairs and cleanup.
  5. Ask the adjuster before discarding damaged items, unless they are a health hazard.

Questions to ask your insurer before you need them

  • How does my policy define sudden and accidental water damage?
  • What is my deductible for a water claim, and does it differ from other claims?
  • Do I have sewer or drain backup coverage, and what is the limit?
  • Does the policy pay for tearing out walls or floors to reach a leaking pipe?
  • What do I have to do, and how fast, to protect the property after a loss?

Reading the policy in advance is far easier than reading it at midnight with water on the floor. Many owners are surprised that a policy treats a burst pipe and a slow leak so differently, and that sewer backup is often an optional add-on.

How coverage and restoration fit together

A restoration company removes water and dries the structure, and an insurer decides what it will pay. The two do not control each other. We explain scope and price before work starts, we keep drying logs and photographs, and we leave coverage decisions to you and your insurer.

If you are comparing options, remember that prompt mitigation is usually part of what a policy expects from you. Waiting days to start drying can make the damage larger and can give an insurer a reason to dispute part of a claim. Starting promptly and documenting well is the safest path.

What California says and what to check in Hollister

The California Department of Insurance, in its residential property claims guide, states that homeowners policies do not cover flood damage and that flood insurance can be bought as a separate policy. It also says other water damage may be covered, for example rain entering through a hole created by a covered event such as strong wind.

Hollister sits near the San Benito River and creeks, so a flood question is worth asking. To see whether your address is in a mapped flood hazard area, check the FEMA flood map for your address, and ask your insurer or agent how that affects your coverage options.

Questions homeowners ask

Does insurance cover water damage from a burst pipe?

Often, when the burst is sudden and accidental. Ask your insurer how it treats the pipe repair versus the resulting damage.

Is a slow leak covered?

Often not. Policies commonly exclude damage from gradual leaks, so report suspected leaks as soon as you find them.

Do I need a separate policy for flooding?

California Department of Insurance guidance says homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, so flood coverage is a separate policy.

Does my Hollister homeowners policy cover water from a rising creek or river?

Usually that falls under flood, which standard homeowners policies do not cover. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer about flood coverage.
